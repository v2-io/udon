## The short version

**The spine works.** With a parser-owned column stack and three lexer conventions the PEG doesn't state yet (below), the grammar parses Joseph's nesting example the way the cards draw it:

```udon
|element-1 |element-2 |element-3 |element-4
                                   child of element-4
                 child of element-2 (sibling of |element-3)
              same, but now with a dedent warning.
  now element-2 is closed as well...
```
```text
root
  |element-1
    |element-2
      |element-3
        |element-4
          prose 'child of element-4'
      prose 'child of element-2 (sibling of |element-3)' / 'same, but now with a dedent warning.'
    prose 'now element-2 is closed as well...'
```

The `|table |tr |td A1` aligned-column idiom, the `|a |b |c |d` / `|child-of-c` / `|child-of-a` case, fences as attribute values, closer comments, nested fences and the top-level column −1 all came out right as well.

**As written, though, much ordinary lite parses into something else, and silently.** In the harness, an attribute line, a sameline element, a comment line inside prose, a sibling element after prose, `:a \` at the end of a line, `:x @jw`, an unclosed fence and a fence closer with trailing text all turn into prose. None of them gives an error or a warning. Nearly all of it comes from one mechanism. `_block`'s last choice is `prose`, and nothing stops the parser from backtracking into it, so any block that fails for any reason becomes text. That is the opposite of what the PEG's own Conventions say ("a start that passes its guard is never backtracked into text"), and it is the failure lite exists to prevent: the same document would mean something different once the bug is fixed. Most of the other defects can't be seen until this one is fixed, because they all end the same way.

I wrote a repaired variant, `peg_repair.txt`, which is eleven local edits plus a stub for the open `_values-below`. With it, every case in the two batteries comes out as its card draws it, or as the error its card names, except the ones listed under "What the repairs don't reach" (§5). The repairs are listed under each finding, written in the PEG's own notation. They are suggestions, and the names in them are placeholders.

## 1. Findings in the grammar, most consequential first

Each finding shows the input, what the PEG gives, what the cards want, and the cause. "Becomes prose" means the whole line, and sometimes the lines under it, ends up as a «text» child.

### 1.1 Failure falls through to prose, never to an error (systemic)

`_block <- BLANK / comment-block / element / attribute-line / fence / ERROR-reserved / prose` (line 25). When `element` fails after `|name` has passed its guard, PEG tries the next choices and `prose` takes the line. The same happens inside a line: if `_line-rest` can't reach `EOL`, the whole `element` fails, and that again ends in prose. Inputs that do this, besides the ones in 1.2–1.8:

| Input | PEG | Cards |
|---|---|---|
| `\|el :a "x""y"` | prose | two values, warning ([[rule:adjacent-quoted-strings]]) |
| `\|café :naïve 1` (with my ASCII stand-in for `LABEL`) | prose | whatever the labels discussion decides, but never prose |
| `\|1 not an element`, `\|-` | prose | open (the guard), but the Conventions say an error |
| `\|a ```` with no closer | prose | error ([[rule:unclosed-delimiter-is-error]]) |

The `%% commit` notes cover only the bracketed forms inside a line. Nothing commits at block level.

*Repair (verified):* `_block <- BLANK / comment-block / &element-start (element / ERROR-bad-element) / &attribute-start (attribute-line / ERROR-bad-attribute) / &fence-start fence / ERROR-reserved / prose`. In other words, a cut after each start's guard. With it, every row above is an error except `"x""y"`. That one becomes an error too, rather than the two values its card's lean wants. Getting the card's reading needs a separator rule that lets a value follow a closing quote directly. That rule is the open `"x""y"` / `"abc"def` question.

### 1.2 Every attribute line becomes prose

`_values-below <- "OPEN"` (line 63) is a literal: it matches only the four characters `OPEN`. So `attribute-line` can never match, and every attribute line falls to prose by 1.1. That includes the case Joseph called "a crucial real-world use reason" (2026-09-29):

```udon
|term Lexicon Entry
  :added <2026-10-01>
  :status frozen
```
The PEG gives `term`, `$main ‹Lexicon Entry›`, then a prose child `:added <2026-10-01>\n:status frozen`. The same happens to top-level attributes ([[rule:top-level-attributes]], ruled "emphatic"), so `:title My Notes` as a document's first line is text.

*Repair:* an open slot needs a placeholder that matches without consuming anything (I used `_values-below <- !"\u0000"`), and a `%%` note saying it is a stub.

### 1.3 A sameline element can never match: two blocks need the same line end

`element <- element-start head _line-rest EOL _blocks EOB` (line 39). In `|a |b`, `b` is an item of `a`'s `_line-rest`. `b` consumes the `EOL`, then its `_blocks` and its `EOB`. Then `a` needs an `EOL` too, and none is left. So `|a |b` fails, and by 1.1 becomes prose. The fence (line 68) and the repaired `attribute-line` hit the same thing. This is the "one PEG tangle" an earlier agent noted in the verbatim record (2026-10-21). It is still in the grammar.

It only works if the line end can be shared. The old descent grammar does exactly that: a sameline child returns to its parent at the start of the next line with that line's column already counted (`|default | col = COL - 1 |>> :dispatch ; construct returned mid-line`), and the parent never reads the newline again. With `EOL` replaced by `_line-end <- EOL / BOL` (where `BOL` is "at the start of a line": zero-width, and true only once a nested block has already taken the line end), all the sameline cases pass. That includes `|el :x |em hi :y 2`, `|nav :aria-label Site |a :href / \ Home` and the fence on an element's line.

*So:* either the lexer table says `EOL` is shared, or the productions use `_line-end`. As the table reads now ("`EOL`: a line ending"), any faithful transcription of the PEG rejects every sameline element.

### 1.4 Prose swallows the structure under it

`prose <- prose-line (prose-line / BLANK)* EOB` (line 73). A second or later prose line is never checked for a start, so prose takes every later line inside its owner, whatever it begins with. Joseph's own example from 2026-10-21:

```udon
|p
  first line
  ; a note
  |a :href https://www.google.com \ Google

  :some-attribute  yeah, I know this will cause a warning.
  now some more prose etc.
```
The PEG gives `|p` with one prose child holding all six lines. The cards, and his correction that day ("You're putting a child (comment) node inside of text when it's a child of |p -- peer to the other children"), give a text, a comment child, the element `a`, the attribute `some-attribute` (with the late-attribute warning), and another text. [[rule:deeper-marker-lines-are-text]] makes a marker line text only when it is *deeper* than the established left edge. At the edge it is structure. The `%%` note on line 74 says so in prose; the production doesn't do it.

*Repair (verified on his example, which then comes out as above):*
```
prose <- prose-line (BLANK* (DEEPER / !(IND _block-start)) IND prose-line)* ;
_block-start <- ";" !"{" / element-start / attribute-start / fence-start / reserved-start ;
```
`IND` is the leading spaces of a continuation line, which no production consumes now (see 2.2). `DEEPER` is a lexer fact: this line sits deeper than the text's *current* left edge. Two things came out of making this work:

- **The trailing `EOB` has to go.** Prose now ends either at a dedent or at a structure line at its edge. The second is not an end of block, so `prose … EOB` fails on it.
- **`DEEPER` has to use the edge as it moves**, which my harness doesn't do. In `|el` / `    deep` / `  shallow` / `   |x`, `shallow` moves the edge to column 2 with a warning, so `|x` at column 3 is deeper and is text. The harness compares against the first line's edge (4), so it makes `|x` an element. That is wrong by [[rule:text-shallower-line]] and [[rule:deeper-marker-lines-are-text]] together. The lexer has to carry the moving edge.

### 1.5 `:a \` with nothing after it becomes prose

[[rule:backslash-empty-text]]'s Statement: "`:name \` with nothing else MUST give the attribute an empty text." `|el :a \`, `:a \` and `|el x \` all become prose. `empty-text <- &BS-SEP` leaves the `BS-SEP` in place. `_line-rest`'s `(_sep _item)*` then takes it as a separator, finds no item at the line end, and backs out, so `EOL` faces ` \` and fails. The card's example `|el :a \ :b ''` passes only because an item follows.

*Repair (verified):* `_line-rest <- (_sep _item)* BS-SEP? framed-comment?`.

### 1.6 Fence errors become prose

- **A closer with trailing text.** [[rule:fence-closer-trailing-text-is-error]]'s own example (`|doc` / ```` ```md ```` / ```` ```js ```` / ```` ``` ````), and ```` ``` not the closer ````, both become prose, along with the fence's body. The `%%` note at line 71 states the error, but `fence-closer <- FENCE-CLOSE framed-comment? EOL` has no branch that produces it.
- **A fence with no closer** becomes prose (1.1).

*Repair (verified):* `fence <- fence-start head _line-rest _line-end (FENCE-BODY fence-closer / ERROR-unclosed-fence)` and `fence-closer <- FENCE-CLOSE framed-comment? (EOL / ERROR-closer-trailing-text)`.

### 1.7 The closer's column doesn't take part in nesting

[[rule:fence-closer-is-a-line]]: the closer's "column MUST then act as any line's column does for nesting", and its cost line says "A closer at column 0 under a deep sameline chain closes the whole chain."

```udon
|a |b ```txt
x
```
     |c
```
By the card, the closer at column 0 closes `b` and `a`, so `c` is a child of the root. The PEG gives `c` as a child of `b`, because nesting is decided only by the next line that isn't blank, and the closer line is already inside `fence`. A lexer can do this: it sees the closer line, so it can emit the `EOB`s for it. But the lexer table doesn't list it, and the `%%` note at line 71 reads as if the grammar already did it. *Not repaired in my variant.*

### 1.8 Reserved spellings: one missed, several reserved that the cards leave as text

- **Missed:** `|el :x @jw` becomes prose. [[rule:reserved-spellings]] makes `@` followed by a name an error "as a value". `attribute` has no reserved branch in its value position. (`|el !if x` is caught, because `_item` has one.) *Repair (verified):* add `/ WS ERROR-reserved` to `attribute`'s choices.
- **Reserved here, text by the cards:** `reserved-start <- ("@" / "!") &(NAME-FIRST / ":" / "[" / "." / "<")` (line 37) applies everywhere it is tested, prose line starts included. The card's table reserves only these:

  - `!` followed by a name or `:`, at a line start and on element and attribute lines;
  - `@` followed by a name, at a prose line start and as a value;
  - `@` followed by a name, `[`, `.` or `<`, on element and attribute lines.

  So the PEG adds `![`, `!.`, `!<` everywhere, and `@[`, `@.`, `@<`, `@:` at prose line starts. The case that matters is Markdown's image syntax:

  ```udon
  |p
    ![the chart](c.png)
  |p ![the chart](c.png)
  ```
  Both lines are errors under the PEG. By the cards both are text (the first is a prose line; [[rule:prose-passes-through]]'s own example has `![the chart](c.png)` mid-line). `@[key] text` at a prose line start is an error here too, and text by the card. [[obj:markdown-text-passes-through]] lists the known Markdown collisions, and this isn't one of them.
- **`null`:** `|el :b null` gives the text `null`. [[rule:nil-spelling]] is ruled ("I agree, rule"): it's an error that points to `nil`. The `%%` note on line 96 says so; no production does it. *Repair (verified):* `ERROR-null <- "null" &BOUND`, tried before `text`.

### 1.9 An attribute line can't hold a later attribute or element

Even with 1.2 stubbed, `attribute-line` ends `… (_sep value framed-comment?)+ … EOL` with no `_line-rest`. So nothing but values may follow on an attribute's own line. That breaks:

- [[rule:line-scan]]'s own Explanation, which is also Joseph's 2026-09-29 `$main` example:
  ```udon
  |element
     :attr this value :another <34>
  ```
  Here the PEG gives a prose child. The card wants `attr ‹this value›` and `another <34>`, "Both lines give …".
- `:c 1 |x` (an element after a finished value, on an attribute line).

*Repair (verified for the first):* give `attribute-line` the same `_line-rest`. That raises a question for Joseph, which is in 4.2.

### 1.10 Missing-value is decided before the lines below are looked at

`ERROR-missing-value <- &(WS attribute-start / EOL)`. Its `EOL` branch fires whenever an attribute has nothing more on its line, so the slot for values below (`_values-below`) is never reached. [[rule:missing-value]]'s Statement makes it an error only with "nothing after it on its line *and nothing indented under it*". Its card [[rule:nested-attributes]] also shows this one as fine:

```udon
|book
  :author
    |person
      :name Jane
```
The PEG gives a missing-value error at `:author`. The `:port` / `:nested 1` case there is an error under both, but the PEG reports the wrong one (missing value at 2:7, where the card has "nested attributes are reserved" at 3:5). The `rule:attribute-bodies` gap can't be filled without moving this branch to after the lines below. *Not repaired;* the stub just inherits the error.

### 1.11 A `%%` note contradicts its production

Line 93: "BOUND is WS, EOL or ] so that `7apples` is an error rather than a text and `nilly` is a text". The productions make `7apples` a text: `number` fails on the bound, and `text` takes it. Either the note or the grammar should change. Which one is the bare-typing gap.

### 1.12 `:a :b 1` gets the missing-value error, not the nested-attribute one

It is an error either way, so this is about the message only: [[rule:attribute-after-attribute-with-no-value]] says Joseph wants this error "not as a deletion detector … but due to nested attributes being reserved". The PEG's own note (line 60) says so; the production reuses `ERROR-missing-value`.

## 2. What the lexer has to do that its table doesn't say

The table (lines 122–139) is where an implementer will look, and the harness needed four duties it doesn't list.

1. **Shared line ends** (1.3): after a block begun mid-line has taken the line end, its enclosing blocks see "start of line" in its place.
2. **Leading spaces of continuation lines.** "`WS` … never a line's leading spaces", and no token takes them. `prose-line <- "\\"? …` can see a leading `\` only if something has stripped the indentation first. `_block-start` at the edge has the same need (1.4). I used `IND`.
3. **The moving left edge** (`DEEPER`, 1.4), and the owner's column as prose's `EOB`. The `EOB` row half-says the second: "For prose the owner's column is what counts".
4. **`RAW` has three different extents** under one name:
   - inside `<…>`, `@{…}` and `;{…}`, up to the matching closer on the line;
   - after a framed `;`, to the end of the line, and it must *not* take the line end, or `_line-rest … EOL` fails;
   - in `comment-block <- ";" !"{" RAW EOB`, which has no `EOL`, so `RAW` must take the line end and every deeper line.

   The table's single row ("a comment's rest and deeper lines; inside `<…>` …") covers the first and third, and contradicts the second. It also leaves [[rule:framed-comment-owns-deeper-lines]] (lines past a framed `;` column belong to the comment) with nowhere to go. In the harness, `|el2 ; note` / `         more` makes `more` a prose child of `el2`, where the card has the comment's text with a warning.

**On who owns the column stack** (lines 141 and 15). My harness is the parser-owned option: `EOB` is a check against a stack of open columns, plus the three small lexical facts above. That was enough for every multi-line case I tried, except the closer's column (1.7) and the moving edge (1.4), both of which are easy to add. The lexer-owned option is feasible. As far as I can see, every start, string, span and bracket is decidable from left to right without backtracking, once the cuts of 1.1 are in. But a lexer that knows where every column-bearing start is has to recognize quoted strings, keys, lists, `<…>`, embeds and the starts' guards. That is most of this PEG's line layer, written a second time. That's an observation about where the cost lands, not a recommendation either way. Descent's choice, the children loop owning the columns, avoids writing it twice.

## 3. The capture convention, and some notation

The three-kind convention (lines 8–12) gives these results when applied literally (harness, strict mode):

- **Characters are lost when a rule has some captured children.** "Its value is its captured children or, if none, its matched characters." `bare-string <- (escape / !(…) .)+`: in `|el :c x\:y z` the value is just the `escape`, and `x`, `y z` are gone. The same happens to `prose-line` when the line holds an embed: `… or @{faq}.` keeps only the reference. Wrapping the plain stretches in a captured rule fixed it in the harness (`chars <- CHAR+`). The other fix is to define the value as captured children and uncaptured runs, interleaved.
- **A framed comment loses its text.** `framed-comment <- WS framed-semicolon (WS RAW / &EOL)` captures only `framed-semicolon` (`;`). The comment's words are `RAW`, uppercase, so they're dropped.
- **The convention's own example doesn't come out as stated.** "`trait <- WS* "." LABEL` captures the label's characters". `LABEL` is uppercase, so `trait` has no captured child and its value is all its matched characters: ` .x`, with the space and the dot.
- **Spelling tokens become AST fields.** `element-start`, `attribute-start` and `framed-semicolon` are lowercase, so each `|`, `:` and `;` turns into a field. `dq-char` is captured, so a string becomes one node per character. `angle`, `reference` and `inline-comment` capture their delimiters, so `<2026-01-01>` keeps its `<` and `>`, while a «delimited-string»'s quotes aren't captured.

**Notation.** `"x" "[0-7]" HEX` and the like use a quoted terminal to stand for a character class. That is the railroad file's convention (it says so in its preamble). The PEG file's Conventions don't mention it, so mermaid and a transcriber read `"[0-7]"` as the five literal characters. I didn't recheck the "parses under mermaid 12.2" claim.

## 4. Spec-level probes for Joseph

These came out of running the cards' rules together. Each one is a composite example with the tree the current rules give, for him to check against how obvious it feels.

### 4.1 One sameline value per attribute, combined with typing by spelling

His 2026-10-21 lean ("perfectly fine with sameline attributes closing after the first value closes I think -- we didn't really have that full conversation in depth yet") plus typing by spelling gives:

```udon
|book :title War and Peace :year 1869
|book :title 1984 is a novel
|item :count 7 apples :price 9.99 USD
|el :alpha true story
```
```text
book   title ‹War and Peace›   year 1869
book   title 1984              $main ‹is a novel›
item   count 7   $main ‹apples›   price 9.99   $main ‹USD›
el     alpha true              $main ‹story›
```
Whether an attribute keeps the rest of its words depends on whether the first word happens to be a number or `true`, `false` or `nil`. [[rule:value-line-escapes]]'s proposed `\7 apples` exists for exactly this, but a writer has to know to reach for it. (Without the one-value lean, the words would stack on the attribute instead: `count` gets `7` and `‹apples›`. That is still a type split, but it stays on the attribute.)

### 4.2 The same lean against "pretend each sameline part is on its own line"

```udon
|el :c 1 z
|el
  :c 1 z
```
Under the lean, the first gives `c 1` and `$main ‹z›`, and the second gives `c` the two values `1` and `‹z›`. [[rule:lines-follow-sameline-columns]] rests on his teaching rule (2026-07-28: "just pretend the same-line part has each of its parts on consecutive new lines"). That rule predicts one tree for both lines, and on 2026-10-21 he described it the same way: "It would be appropriate from a pure linefeed perspective as if `:c 1 z` [were] on its own line". Either the teaching rule covers elements only, or the lean breaks it. The repair in 1.9 raises the matching question one level down: in `:a 1 :b 2 z` on an attribute's own line, does `z` stack on `b`, stack on `a`, or go nowhere?

### 4.3 A framed backslash with nothing before it

```udon
|p \ tail |{em x} more
|el :a \ x
```
The PEG treats the first ` \ ` as a plain separator, so `p` gets `$main ‹tail |{em x} more›`. The second gives `a ‹›` (empty text, [[rule:backslash-empty-text]]) and `$main ‹x›`. "It only ends a text" says nothing about an element's head with no text before the backslash. Is it allowed, an error, or an empty `$main`?

### 4.4 A Markdown table flush with its paragraph

```udon
|doc
  Results:
  |Name|Role|
  |----|----|
```
`Results:` sets the edge at column 2, and the rows sit *at* the edge, not deeper, so by the cards they are structure. With the cuts of 1.1, `|Name|Role|` is an error. As written it becomes prose, which is what a Markdown writer wants, but only by accident. The objective names tight pipe tables as a known collision. What it doesn't say is that indenting the table under a paragraph doesn't help unless the rows go deeper than the paragraph. Whether `|Name|` with a `|` right after the name may count as "not a start" is the guard question in the labels discussion. It can't be settled by looking one or two characters ahead, which matters for the lexer-ownership condition on line 141.

### 4.5 ` :word` in ordinary sameline text

`|q :says Do it :now please` gives `says ‹Do it›` and an attribute `now ‹please›`. That follows from the ruled [[rule:text-value-end]]; I list it only as a hazard for [[expl:common-mistakes]]. ` :-)` and ` :1` stay text through the guard.

## 5. What the repairs don't reach

- The closer's column (1.7), the moving edge (1.4), and comment ownership of deeper lines after a framed `;` (2, item 4).
- `"x""y"` and `"abc"def`: these become errors with the cuts, where the card's lean wants two values with a warning.
- `_values-below` (1.10), which is the `rule:attribute-bodies` gap.
- Warnings in general (late attributes, the shallower-line warning, top-level dedent): the harness doesn't model them.
- Tabs. `|a :x some⇥value :y 1` parses under every variant, but [[rule:column-shifting-whitespace-error]] makes it an error. The table gives this to the lexer (`WS`), which means the lexer has to know that a start follows later on the line. That is one more piece of line-level knowledge the lexer would need (§2).
- In the repaired variant, `:a 1 :b 2 z` on an attribute's own line sends `z` to a `main` capture. That is the open question in 4.2, not an answer to it.

## 6. Scope, and what this audit didn't do

- My interpreter is my own reading of `railroad-peg-beta`'s semantics. It has packrat memoization. An `ERROR-…` production raises an error the moment it matches, even inside a branch that would otherwise be abandoned. `%% commit` is applied to the rules whose notes say so. The PEG doesn't state the first of these choices; it should.
- My stand-ins for the open classes: `LABEL` = `[A-Za-z_][A-Za-z0-9_-]*`, `NAME-FIRST` = `[A-Za-z_]`, `NUMBER` = `[-+]?\d+(\.\d+)?`, and `ESCAPABLE` = the coordinator's proposed set. Findings that rest on them (`|café`, `.5`, `1.0.3`) are about the fall-through, not about the classes.
- I didn't check the tree builder's job: joining text lines, dedent, `$key`/`$traits` desugaring, where comments attach. The harness shows captures only.
- I read 10 of the 38 def cards. A finding that turns on a term's meaning should be checked against the rest.
- The list is what I found, not everything there is to find. I tested 99 case blocks (`cases.txt`, `cases2.txt`, many with several lines each), the two multi-line files, and about 30 one-off probes, and the cases outside them (embed interiors, lists inside keys, value-mode lines below an attribute) are thin or absent.

## 7. The harness

`.int/peg-audit-2026-10-21/`:

- `pegi.py`: the interpreter. It reads the mermaid block (`peg.txt`, copied from the PEG at `6b88240`) and models the lexer tokens. Modes: `line` and `block`, each optionally `-shared` (the shared line end of 1.3).
- `summ.py`: prints a capture tree in a card-like shape.
- `runall.py`: runs `cases.txt` or `cases2.txt` (`C=`) against a grammar (first argument).
- `peg_fix.txt`: the PEG with only `_values-below` stubbed. `peg_repair.txt`: the repairs above (`diff peg.txt peg_repair.txt` shows all of them).
