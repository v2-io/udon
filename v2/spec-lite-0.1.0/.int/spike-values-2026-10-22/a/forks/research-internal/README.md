# Joseph's own past thinking on values, types, notations, units and `<…>` (research fork, spike a)

*A fork of spike a's coordinator (Opus 5.5), 2026-10-22. It records **Joseph's own words**, dated, from `~/.claude/history.jsonl` (his typed prompts), with a few v2 documents for context, and weighs each against `CTQ-FACTORS.md`. Per his note relayed mid-spike, nothing here counts as precedent in itself. What it records is what he thought and when. Estate documents are not usage data. Agent-written spec text is marked as such and weighted lower than his words.*

**Joseph's two notes, applied here.** (1) CTQ-FACTORS is the compass, and his past words are a record of what he thought, not an authority. (2) The estate's documents and analyses were written by fiat and are ideation, not evidence of how anyone writes or reads. So three things cited below count as ideas to weigh, never as evidence: the agent analyses (MINEFIELD-MAP, dialects-ideation), the 0.9.1 spec text, and the 07-28 cross-model testimony, which is agents' instincts under a primed query.

**How it was gathered (checked).** I ran a scan of `history.jsonl` (22,972 prompts) for prompts in udon projects or mentioning udon that contain type, notation, unit, dialect, slot, schema, `<…>`, numeric or temporal vocabulary. That gave 589 prompts, and I read the ~325 that hit the narrower vocabulary, plus full text for ~35 of them. I also did a second scan across all projects for type-system vocabulary (algebraic, alias, uom, significant figures, canonical). I did not read the 0.10.x drafts, the greenfield archives or `v2/theory` whole, only the passages cited. Line numbers below are `history.jsonl` lines.

## 2. What the record does to F1 and the decision points

**F1 (streaming doesn't separate B from C-with-the-view): the record sharpens it.**

- The streaming objection has decided the bare-typing question twice, in opposite directions. On 2026-07-15 it chose **A**: "typed by the initial digit … would need potentially unlimited lookahead to know it should be text. *I* vote that this example actually errors out." On 2026-10-21 it chose **C**: `4938…p`. Both times the cost being avoided is the same, the holdback before "it should be text" is known. Units moved him from A to C ("in notation there are units all the time"). Streaming never moved him toward B. If F1 holds (the classification is structurally inert under the bare-run rule, so it can be a trailer), the cost that drove both moves was a property of the event protocol, not of B.
- **He has accepted kind-at-the-end before** (L7711, 2026-01-10): "Without lookahead … the host … will do some "inner" parsing and coercion itself." The old machine emitted tag plus slice when the token ended. So F1's trailer is close to what he signed off on in January, not new machinery. What's new is emitting the characters before the tag.
- **What he gave up on the way to C** (L22733, the morning of the same day): "the magic of udon values", meaning bare `3` is numeric. If F1 holds, the reason he gave it up no longer applies. That makes it a question to put back to him in his own words ("on 10-21 you called bare `3` → numeric 'the magic of udon values', and gave it up for streaming; if streaming doesn't separate them, do you still want C?"), not one for the spike to settle.
- **A caution F1 must meet.** The CTQ wording of B1 is "Every construct's kind is known within a few characters." F1 needs "kind" there to mean *construct* kind (a bare run), with the classification as a later attribute of it. That reading is the spike's to argue and his to accept. The record shows him treating "type" and "kind" interchangeably (`::kind.form`, 10-20).
- An agent-written precedent for F1's split (v2 `theory/to-integrate/refine-more/dialects-ideation/README.md`, 2026-07-28, not his words): "Capture (where a value ends)" vs "Recognize (is this body a Date)", with "core owns *shape*, dialect owns *meaning*" as a middle branch. F1 says a bare run's classification is a Recognize job that never touches Capture.

**DP1 (where the classification lives).** His words run, in order: syntax-parsed types (Dec), classify and never convert (Jan), A (July), the frozen bare set plus envelope (July, 0.9.1, ratified in spirit), "magic of udon values" (10-21 morning), then C-with-the-view (10-21 evening). The constant across all of them is "by spelling, never sniffing". **Against CTQ:** an agent's analysis (`v2/theory/to-integrate/primary/format-failures/MINEFIELD-MAP.md` §M1, derived, not his words) states the safety boundary precisely: "Sniffing is safe iff the bare syntactic classes are pairwise disjoint". The frozen set is non-injective only over "four keywords plus the numeric literals". C makes bare space injective in the tree. C-with-the-view and a class attachment move the non-injectivity out of the tree into the default reading. That is where B3 (one tree in every parser) and A2/A3 (silent misses) should be weighed.

**DP2 (membership).** His wants, constant since July: bases, `_`, and scientific notation as "literal qualifiers" that let "the udon reflect the literal value without the agent/user needing to translate it from a natural representation". This is A5 domain-forward, in his words. Rationals "want to be a composition of two literals", so they go to a dialect. `i` is "borderline". Temporal goes in brackets. Domain needs are named: constants, rust types, temporal, uom, significant figures. **Additivity constraint (0.9.1 CORE §11.1, agent text built on his July rulings):** "Nothing is ever added to bare recognition; this is what makes dialect growth structurally unable to retype existing documents." Any bare class (quantity, say) has to be in the first lite or never. A class attachment softens this, since adding a class changes an attachment and not the value, but it still changes the default reading.

**DP3 (identifier safety).** No words of his on zip codes or leading zeros were found. The only identifier-shaped statement is the port remark (10-21: text passed along "instead of for its mathematical value" is fine). The spec line "`0755` is `755`" descends from the Dec 2025 SPEC. MINEFIELD §3.1 (agent) asks for one sentence on whether leading zeros are lexically preserved, and notes that 0.8 TIME-SPEC once warned on near-miss literals, a precedent for warning instead of silently retyping.

**DP4 (what `<…>` is for).** His words give it at least five roles, in this order:
1. "this is not text": an escape from attribute plain-text (07-08, 07-12), so that prose-space doesn't erode;
2. a dialect envelope, dispatched by declared dialects in declared order (07-12, 07-23);
3. a way to force a primitive "if there was ambiguity" (10-20);
4. a value as a value, not a referent: `@<ref>` vs `@{ref}` (10-11), `@{<…>}` (10-20), "`<"A string">` is a string type" (08-07);
5. `<>` as nil (07-18 for current behavior, 10-22 as a feature).

He called role 4's current use "a little unprincipled" (10-22). **A reading the record suggests (mine, not his):** roles 1, 3 and 4 share one meaning, *the thing inside is a datum, taken as a value and not as text or a referent*. That is close to his distinction 1 (Lisp's quote). Role 2 (dispatch) is the only one that needs a name inside. If that holds, `<…>` is lite's value bracket, and the type name inside is an optional refinement of it. **Tension with outside testimony:** unprimed cross-model agents in 2026-07-28 (`v2/udon-needs/…/de-novo-testimony/dialects-testimony-grok-2026-07-28.md`, agent instincts, with a query that was itself primed toward envelopes) rejected disambiguation by load order: "Disambiguation should be in the document or in an explicit type mark … not in load order". That cuts against his 07-12 "declared order; first claim wins" and his 07-23 "can probably be reduced … as long as there is no ambiguity".

**DP5 (naming a notation).** Always a prefix ("decide and start parsing sooner", 10-22). The separator has never been settled: `<u64i|0xf902>` (07-12), `<time:interval:…>` (07-12), `<core:temporal:timespan 3m5d>` (07-23, space before the body), `<uuid7:…>` (08-08), `<uint32|0x992BC>` (10-21), `<dt:…>`, `<rust:i64: 0x73DDF>`, `:when:dt(YY-MM-DD)<27-03-05>` (10-22). A ladder from unlabeled to `type:` to `dialect:type:` has appeared every time since July. Parameters on a notation (`dt(YY-MM-DD)`) appear only on 10-22.

**DP6 (slot types): the most consistent thread in the record.** "Attributes keep track of the label from the parent perspective, *and* its type implicitly (or, soon, explicitly)" (07-15). Dialect "adds the types (necessary as it typechecks attributes)" (08-08). Algebraic names: `port` = u16 | standard-port (10-22). Across three months the slot, not the literal, is where he puts a type, and the attribute name *is* the slot. With A10 devalued and A5 raised in the CTQ (10-22), the record and the factors point the same way: slot types come from schema or convention, and literal marks are for disambiguation. The record has nothing on spelling a slot type inline, except his 10-22 `:when:iso8601 …` imaginings.

**DP7 (the trailing `:`).** Two older uses of his rhyme with it. In 2011 DECIDED.md: "':' allowed in labels" and "if you want an attribute starting with a colon (ala ruby labels)". Ruby's labels are the trailing-colon form `role:`. In 2025-12 (L6058): `!:json: …`, "you start the "inner language" immediately after the second ':' (which would emit the raw label/namespace)". In both, a colon-closed label introduces what follows. So the trailing `:` has a lineage in his words as a **closer of a label before a body in another language or notation**. That bears on whether `:when:dt …` reads as "attribute `when`, in notation `dt`". It collides with `:` inside bare labels (`xml:lang`), which 2011 allowed and the 10-20 lean keeps.

**DP8 (nil).** `<>` (and whitespace-only `< >`) as nil goes back to 2026-07-18, so it predates 10-22. Lists are the stated exception (an empty list, not a list holding nil).

**DP9 (units).** "920px being an error violates least surprisal" (10-21); "`<5m>`" bracketed shorthand (07-12); uom and significant figures (07-14); the 2011 attic's "number with unit (e.g., 18px)" and "float-with-kind 29.5px". His constant: the literal should be written in its natural notation (A5). Significant figures need the spelling kept exactly (`1.10` ≠ `1.1`), which favors any design where the tree keeps the spelling and conversion is a reader's job.

**DP10 (aliases, algebraic names).** Only in schema and dialect talk (08-08 "algebraic types"; 07-29 type-algebra spike; 10-22 `port`). Lite owes this layer nothing beyond a tree that keeps the attribute name and the spelling. `:port ssh` and `:port 22` must both arrive at it intact.

## 3. Where his thinking moved, and why (for weighing, not deference)

- **The July choice to put every temporal value in `<…>` was made for two reasons:** to avoid eroding prose-space, and least surprise (07-08). Under C (bare is always text) the first reason disappears, because a bare date can't erode anything. The 10-22 CTQ (A5 up, A10 down) then favors the slot over the per-value bracket. So the July rule is a candidate for reversal on his own current factors: dates bare, typed by slot, with `<…>` only when the author must say so. This is my inference.
- **The October view of `<…>` as "a value more generically" (10-22)** is a generalization of July's "this is not text", by way of `@<ref>` (10-11). The trajectory points toward a value bracket, not a temporal envelope.
- **Rejected or abandoned by him:** A (for notation, because of units); `null` as a second nil spelling (10-09); declared-order dispatch has not been revisited since July and is challenged only by unprimed testimony; homoiconicity as beauty (10-22).
