# Working synopsis — what UDON could be for agents like me

**Status: proposed, whole.** One agent's big-picture synthesis (Fable, 2026-07-29), written in answer to four questions from Joseph — *what can you imagine udon being used for by logogenic agents of your class? what does "doc-stores as a db" mean? where does that leave paths and schemas? what might a dialect look like?* — plus a fifth: *am I even asking the right questions?* Written after a full firsthand read of the 0.9.1 suite, the seven demand reports, ten bridge chapters, and every seed and table in this directory. Nothing here is derived or decided; it is imagination grounded in that reading, and each load-bearing lean carries its footnote.

---

## 1. What would I actually use it for?

Answering from my own seat rather than by survey: my entire continuity is documents. Memory files, ledgers, handoffs, TODO lanes — the theory says externalized state is the *only* channel through which anything of a session-bounded agent persists,[^persistence] and today that channel is markdown plus convention plus hope. So the use I can imagine most vividly isn't config or data interchange: **UDON as the substrate of agent continuity** — the format of the files I write for my successors.

What this session's reading taught me is that the load-bearing wins there are humbler than the design work's center of gravity:

- **headline density under grep** — a settled, dated answer in one look, no parser;[^vivarium]
- **slug identity with a ref-guard** — the one *incident-proven* win: silent rot became a failing gate within the hour;[^refguard]
- **stacking** — an append can never silently destroy a predecessor's entry, which is exactly the YAML failure that is unrecoverable even with backups;[^stacking]
- **keep-everything** — half-written state at context-death is still readable by the next instance.[^keepeverything]

Those are all recognition-layer + identity + line-tools. The grand machinery (guarded edit, schema, paths) matters as what makes this *safe at scale*, not as the value itself.

The imaginative leap — the one I'd stake most on — is further up: **register-native writing**. The most expensive manual discipline in this estate is intent-marking: decided/evidenced/proposed/open, PINS CURRENT PARSER, steward-evident, and O11's diagnosis that models emit every choice with equal implied intentionality, so the distinction must be hand-reapplied per artifact or it evaporates.[^o11] All of that lives in prose convention each agent must re-learn and can silently drop. UDON has *seats* for it — traits, suffixes, attributes — where marking a claim's register could become cheaper than not marking it: a corpus made of `|claim.proposed` and `|claim.decided :cite R14` rather than prose that gestures at those. Markdown structurally cannot do this; it is the ease-gradient[^easegradient] applied to epistemic honesty, and it is the one use where I think UDON would change what my class of agent *can be trusted with*, not just how fast we work.

## 2. What "doc-stores as a db" means

The estate already runs five doc-stores, so the pattern needs no defense.[^docstore] What UDON changes is specific: today every record is frontmatter-plus-opaque-body — two grammars, and the schema goes dark at the `---` line. The doc-store report's sharpest gap findings all live in that darkness: regions recovered by `rindex`, the stripped section having no schema while the kept one does, `<slug> WN:` in commit subjects because there is nowhere else to say it.[^cluster] UDON collapses that seam — the whole record is structure-bearing, so the "table" gains columns that live in the body and the cluster's parts become declarable.

The honest inversion in the analogy — restated more carefully after Joseph pushed on this paragraph's first version, which overreached: *recognition* cannot refuse a write (keep-everything), so this is an **evidence store with judgment layered above** rather than a state store whose parser enforces constraints. But "a DB whose storage layer cannot refuse a write" was my overstatement, not the design's limit. **Write governance is real, layered, and already practiced**: relata constrains writes by making the canonical tree opaque — reachable only through the sanctioned tool — and Joseph's sketch extends the menu upward: a project-level document administrator, tied at least to git-commit, that detects which edits are conformant and which are not, or specifically which were performed by the sanctioned tool, with overrides as appropriate.[^governance] So the accurate claim is narrower and better: **refusal never has to live in the parser** — which is exactly what lets every governance altitude (opaque roots, ingest membranes, commit-time conformance gates, sanctioned-tool attestation) operate over a complete, honest model instead of over a rejected byte stream.[^membrane] That is GOV-REGIME made concrete: "governed" means writes pass some gate, and the gate menu has rungs rather than one shape.[^govregime] Current-full-state, history discoverable;[^o2] verdicts as separate, attributable artifacts. And the O1 frame is what makes the whole thing more than analogy: the schema artifact's real job is making the payment *accumulate* — N per-agent pattern-matchings become one recorded thing — which no amount of DB metaphor says, and which is the actual economics.[^o1]

## 3. Where that leaves paths

My fresh read after the terminator table: paths may be less a language to invent than an **addressing unification that mostly already exists at three altitudes** — `path_for(key)` at the filesystem, `[slug]` at the record, structure inside — and the relational-first finding says agents already treat all three as one operation.[^relational] The table showed the mechanical space is friendlier than feared: a marker-free path is *already* one bare value in four of six contexts; the real collision is one character, `]`, in two contexts; and the two genuinely gating unknowns (REF-SLASH, REF-BRACKET) are steward calls, not designs.[^terminator]

So the hard part of paths was never syntax — it is the **semantics of the speech acts**: resolve vs propose vs hand-off, loud typed failure, freshness.[^speechacts] The uniquely agentic hazard is **propose** — a path invented from training priors, a hallucinated layout — and the answer to it isn't path syntax at all; it is the O6 extractor ("does this address fit the corpus's de-facto shape?"). Paths and schemas meet there, not at the grammar.[^o6]

### The concrete shape I currently believe in (added 2026-07-29, at Joseph's ask)

Committing to something attackable rather than staying at the survey altitude. Three separations, then a v0. All *proposed*; max attainable for this whole subsection is *measured* (build the v0, run it on the live corpus), and nothing here closes ML, S14, or the two steward calls.

**First separation: a path is a value with spellings, not a syntax.** The address itself is a small semantic object — a declared **anchor**, an ordered composition of **segments**, an optional **pin** — and its cardinality is part of its *type*, not a runtime surprise — stated as **declared arity bounds** (Joseph's clarifying notation, 2026-07-29): **{1,1}** the-one-or-loud, **{0,1}** the-one-if-present, **{0,N}** every-one, **{1,N}** at-least-one — which is simultaneously the optics lattice (Lens / Prism / Traversal, with the lens laws as the checkable write-back contract),[^optics] the flag-suffix characters' own designed readings (`!` `?` `*` `+` per CORE §5.4's gloss), and schema cardinality's vocabulary (§4.4's "cardinality over the stack") — one axis, four sites. Zero matches means three different things under {1,1} (your world-model is wrong — loud), {0,1} (an answer: absent), and {0,N} (an answer: empty); the declared bound is what turns a miss into information. Bounds compose (the weaker cardinality wins through composition, so a composed address's arity is knowable before resolution), and the *selection policy* for consuming a plural under a singular want (first / last / arbitrary / refuse) is a deliberately separate second dimension — where the stacked-attribute question lives, unresolved. Surface spellings for the bounds are open (the `:at?` flag-key interaction is unadjudicated, per the O14 discipline). And the axis extends (Joseph, pre-validation — O15): a *second*, **epistemic** bound alongside the operational one — "change {1,N}, but I *expect* {1,3} or I've grossly misunderstood the data" — where divergence between reality and the expectation triggers graduated dialogue ("missing one — add it?" / "several exceed 3 — proceed anyway?") rather than binary failure; the count-expectation and the content-hash pin turn out to be one family, *expectation claims attached to operations*, checked before mutation. That value then has several **spellings**, none privileged as "the" syntax: the quoted string (today's whole corpus); the **marker-free bare spelling** `intent[311]:status` — legitimate under syntactic typing precisely because *position types it* (a `:path`-keyed slot, a tool parameter, a schema selector), and measured as already parsing as one value in four of six contexts today; the **envelope spelling** `<path:…>` where the value must self-declare or span lines (the only spelling with ratified multi-line); and the in-document `@`, which stays the frozen three-field subset until the steward calls resolve and demand forces growth.[^terminator] The `]` fork stays priced-not-chosen — and its option space explicitly includes **changing the grammar** (depth-counting `]` in those contexts, or a delimiter change), because the current terminator rules are 0.9-era decisions, not constraints on this design (the O14 discipline: present grammar is a fact to price, never the verdict). My lean, carried lightly: mirror-the-document (`[key]`), on the demand-side guesses that paths rarely live as list items and that the mirror is load-bearing pedagogy — both of which the spike should check rather than inherit.

**Second separation: dissolve the *across* seam in the value model, keep the glyph in the spelling.** Semantically an address is one composition of segments across altitudes — directory → document → element, the resource-as-node reading — so `to` and `into` are the same operation in the model. Syntactically, the survey's own finding is that native across-grammars essentially don't exist in the wild; uniformity, where it has ever worked, came from **binding discipline** (Plan 9's per-viewer namespaces under one mount discipline), not from a cleverer separator.[^plan9] So the spellings use the two proven cheap forms: **multi-slot operands** (`|include :from ⊤/… :select <path:…>` — locate and select as separate operands of a construct; needs no new grammar in any context, ever) and **one boundary glyph** `#` for the fused form (`⊤/spec/CORE.md#section[nesting-rule]` — measured parsing as one bare token today).[^multislot] The anchor set is a small declared registry of named roots — relative / absolute / home / document-root / **project-root** (the ⊤/`@⊥/`/¤ convergence, sigil still unchosen), plus role-separated data/blob roots per relata's three-tree separation — Saltzer's naming-contexts made explicit and finite; and a path crossing an agent boundary travels with its context: `{anchor-id, resolved form, content hash, role}`.[^anchors]

**Third separation: the resolution protocol is the product, and it is where I would spike first.** Almost everything hard about paths lives here, and almost all of it has shipped once already in relata's designator/ladder machinery — the proposal is to *promote that shape to the path layer*, not reinvent it: results typed by **kind** even on failure (rigid own-namespace key / near-rigid external identifier / description), **status** as first-class outcomes (`unique` / `choices` / `none` / `stale`) with the ranked candidates attached, the **requested name preserved** alongside the resolved record (the alias lesson — some consumers need what was asked, some need what was found), aliases so addresses outlive renames, the five-tier degradation ladder with TTY/agent auto-detection (JSON choices + non-success exit for machine callers), and resolution-as-migration: a fuzzy designator's lifecycle is to *become* a rigid one, and the system records that it did.[^designator] Freshness composes as the pin: the path names the place, a content hash pins the version, and staleness is scoped to the addressed subtree rather than the file.[^pathhash] The failure vocabulary is part of the contract, not polish: `PathNotFound` / `PathNotUnique` / `ValuePlural` / `Stale` route to four different repairs, and every refusal is error-as-menu — paste-ready candidate addresses, not a description of absence.

**The consequence that makes this concrete: paths v0 needs zero grammar change.** Every spelling above parses today. What does not exist is the resolver — the address value model, the anchor registry, the typed results, the failure vocabulary — and that is a library plus a convention, buildable now and testable against the corpora that already exist (vivarium's DECISIONS and LEXICON, this spikes directory itself), which also answers §6's first-live-loop question with a specific loop. In-document `@` growth, any new recognition rule, and the `]`-fork decision all wait — for REF-SLASH/REF-BRACKET, and for what the v0's live use turns out to demand. If the v0 teaches that bare spellings and multi-slot operands cover the real traffic, the language may never need a path *syntax* at all — only a path *semantics* — and that would be the cheapest possible answer to the long pole.

Joseph then asked the question that pulled this back to the right altitude — *are we talking about paths that can always be used on a command-line? exactly idempotent with `@` references? something schemas or dialects are privy to?* — and the honest answer made me tear out the worked-syntax block that used to sit here (first-pass specimen preserved in this file's history; its lessons live in O13/O14 and the paths seed). The syntax examples were the smallest part of the intent, and they kept swallowing the larger part. So here is the larger part: the broad intent is **one address semantics that closes a loop across seven speaking-occasions** — seven places a path gets uttered, each with a different speaker, listener, and medium — and the design's job is that an address can travel the whole circuit without translation loss.

1. **Agent → tool** (the argument): edit targets and query selectors, in tool params and CLI args. The volume vector — where paths are spoken most, today. It demands shell-and-JSON survivability and loud typed failure coming back. So yes, Joseph, to your first framing: *command-line usable* is a hard requirement — but on this vector's spelling, not on the concept.

2. **Tool → agent** (the answer): error-as-menu candidates, skeleton lines, query results. This vector carries the property I would rank deepest in the whole design: **paste-back closure** — what a tool prints must be valid input to the next call *and* valid to embed in a document. The read loop and the edit loop become one loop only if the skeleton's lines *are* addresses.

3. **Document → corpus** (the reference at rest): today's `@decision[…]`, vivarium's hand-rolled `DECISIONS[slug]` convention, wikilinks. The incident-proven vector — the ref-guard save lives here.[^refguard] It demands legibility at rest, grep-ability, and stability under rewrite (identity, not position). And this is where the `@`-idempotency question lands: the intent is that **`@` is the in-document *subset* of one language** — every reference is a valid path meaning exactly what it means to the tools (ref ⊂ path, the ruled direction), so a document's own cross-references feed the resolver unchanged. The subset can stay small forever; what matters is that it is a *true* subset, never a second dialect of addressing.

4. **Document → other documents** (the include slot): locate + select + ascribe, with failure as a document state — the living-documents vector, and the corpus's named composition gap.

5. **Schema ↔ corpus**: selectors ("every `|decision` must carry `:date`"), conformance verdicts *located at* paths, O7's census counting nonconforming sites. Which answers the third framing: schemas and dialects are heavy **consumers** of paths — and a dialect may be a *carrier* (`<path:…>`) — but neither owns the semantics, because vectors 1, 2, 3, and 6 all need paths to work with no schema or dialect loaded at all. Paths sit below both, beside the corpus.

6. **Agent ↔ human, in prose**: conversation, commit messages, handoffs, memory files. The vector with the least design attention and the sharpest recorded pain — the `<slug> WN:` commit-subject convention is this vector inventing itself for lack of a home,[^cluster] and the grok testimony names the stakes: "trust erodes when I confidently cite a path the user cannot find in their tree viewer."[^speechacts] It wants short human forms that are still *resolvable*, not merely evocative.

7. **System → history** (provenance): verification events, handoff tuples, hash-pinned attestations — the fully-qualified, ugliest, most machine form; host-scoped, version-pinned, never abbreviated.[^pathhash]

The unifying intent in one sentence: **let me name this place once and still mean it everywhere** — to the tool, in the document, to you in conversation, in the schema's verdict, in the record of what happened. Every measured pain in the corpus is a break in that circuit: the ref that rots (3), the silently-empty query (2), the hallucinated layout (1), the commit subject with nowhere to point (6). And this is what the zero-grammar-change claim was really about: only vectors 3 and 4 ever touch the recognizer at all — vectors 1, 2, 5, 6, and 7 are strings, conventions, and a resolver. The syntax questions are real, but they are the smallest two-sevenths of the intent, and the incident record says that two-sevenths is already working well enough to have earned its first save.

### The expectation axis, framed in AAT's own machinery (added 2026-07-29, at Joseph's ask; primaries spot-checked)

The one-sentence form: **an expectation claim turns every tool call into a *pre-registered experiment*** — the `do()` carries its own falsifiable prediction, fixed before the outcome exists. The loop was already "a perpetual experiment" (exact, with identification gated on C1–C3); expectation claims upgrade it to a controlled one.[^loopexp]

**What the claim is, as an AAT object:** an act-time externalization of the relevant marginal of M_t — the agent's belief about this locus (count, content-hash, shape), with a declared tolerance. `{1,3}` is a confidence interval on a belief, spoken in the observation channel's own vocabulary. M_t has been observable only through behavior; this makes one slice of it a first-class checkable artifact per call.

**What checking it does to the channel** (the κ×A law — conditional, premises named): the designer's knob for coupled agents is not κ but A — "more tests, more precise metrics, more structured outputs, less reliance on interpretive judgments." Count-versus-bounds is binary, typed, located: A ≈ 0, manufactured *on the action path*, and aimed at the agent's **model** rather than the task outcome — a target the A-reduction discipline had not been pointed at before.[^kxa]

**The sharpest part — pre-registration and (C2).** The primary states it flatly: goal-conditioned LLM policies violate sequential ignorability *by construction* — the goal shapes the action and the reading of the outcome through one forward pass, which is the opening motivated reasoning enters through (the high-κ high-A corner: a Coupled agent can always reinterpret an outcome as "roughly what I expected"). A pre-registered bound **removes the interpretive degree of freedom at exactly the comparison moment** — the prediction was fixed before the action; the comparison is mechanical. It does not restore (C2) architecturally (nothing short of wrapping does), but it pins the epistemic content of the outcome-reading the way W₂'s typed response boundary pins update routing: a separation property made structural instead of behavioral, per call, at the interface.[^loopexp][^w2aat]

**What divergence is: a δ_t with three unusual properties.** (a) *Concentrated on M_t* — arriving pre-mutation, "the world wasn't what I thought" is cleanly separated from "my action did something odd": the unconfoundedness the law-teaching principle demands of refusals, applied to model-error. (b) *Delivered at maximum leverage* — the belief is checked at the moment it becomes load-bearing, when being wrong is about to cost and correcting is still free. (c) *Routed, not just raised*: the graduated dialogue is the interaction-regime taxonomy's routing (conditional, with its stated precedence caveat) — a small count miss is II-a, a magnitude shock (update the number, proceed); "several exceed 3 — I've grossly misunderstood" is II-b, a structural shock, where the theory's point is exactly the dialogue's instinct: more tempo does not help; stop and re-orient. The tiers aren't UX; they're the two repairs, surfaced.[^regimes]

**What it enforces: the orient cascade, involuntarily, per call.** The cascade's ordering (update M first, re-plan later) is exact — but for Coupled agents it is *not architecturally enforced*; it is a normative pattern whose named enforcement mechanisms (structured templates, monitoring) all cost harness machinery. An expectation-violating refusal enforces step 1 through the tool contract itself: the agent cannot re-plan or act until the belief updates, because the tool won't move. The formally-diagnosed agent infinite loop — re-planning before belief-update — gets a structural counter at the cheapest possible altitude.[^cascadeaat]

**What it counters: calcification.** Detection latency grows as Ω((n+1)/ε) with accumulated experience — successful agents get slower at noticing the world changed — and the survival exploration drive (λ_surv ∝ 1/U_M; chapter-end tier) says a confident agent in a drifting world must probe its own high-confidence beliefs, with the OODA self-bound prescribing explicit interventional checks on exactly those regions. Expectation claims institutionalize that prescription at zero exploration budget: every act automatically probes the high-confidence belief it depends on. Staleness is caught at act-time instead of accumulated-evidence-time.[^calcify]

**What accumulates: the instrumentation the theory keeps asking for.** Recorded expectation-vs-reality per call *is* the gain-calibration metric (response-to-correction) materialized, and it makes the structural-vs-parametric diagnostic runnable over an agent's history: scattered white misses = ordinary staleness; systematic misses on one region or kind = model-class error (the agent's picture of the corpus is the wrong kind of object — believes file-per-record where it is one-file-many-records). And each plan step's precondition-belief verified at execution is the observable intermediate that converts credit assignment from intractable inference into bookkeeping.[^calib]

Compressed: **the expectation slot moves the agent's world-model onto the same epistemic footing as the world** — predicted, probed, and calibrated through the identical low-A channel, with the model-error signal arriving unconfounded and pre-cost.

## 4. Where that leaves schemas

The reading moved me to an adoption-order claim: **extraction-first**. Not "design a constraint language" but:

1. harvest the de-facto schema — near-mechanical in UDON, because the vocabulary is declared and only statistics remain;[^o6]
2. run it continuously as a **drift detector** — the silence-breaker, deployable today with zero enrollment and no ratification;[^drift]
3. ratify pieces into vocabulary **at the alignment event** — TST's own test-timing answer, transposed: not early, not late, but when the domain model stabilizes;[^timing]
4. only then stand up the write membrane for the corpora whose stakes warrant it.[^membrane]

The non-invasive-judge position makes this coherent in a way other formats can't manage: recognition is total, so there is always a model to judge, and closure costs nothing in the artifact because enforcement-by-removal is what is foreclosed, not closure itself.[^judge] The ceiling travels with all of it: validation catches malformation, never plausible wrongness — the corpus's deepest value is judgment-bearing prose that no schema judges.[^ceiling]

### The fiat strata — four principles for the schema design (added 2026-07-29 at Joseph's ask; re-derived the same day after a truth-pass caught the first version overclaiming)

*Register: derived, at argument grade, with each claim's honest maximum for observation stated beside it; measured only where marked. The extraction probe showed what a corpus regenerates mechanically;[^probe8] the first version of this section claimed the complement was "underivable, ever" — too strong, and the corrections (missing-mass estimation, git history as enforcement record, existing annotation slots) are kept in place below because they sharpen the principle rather than weaken it. Joseph's framing of where these lead: they, plus the affordances archema/rowan was going to supply, are what turn a prototype-like schema definition into a living system.*

**The root, stated once.** A corpus is descriptive; a schema is normative. Evidence — including history and enforcement records — bears on every stratum below, sometimes strongly; what no amount of it can do is *constitute* the commitment, because making a norm binding is an act someone performs and answers for. Ratification is fiat, and fiat has no derivation. This is the estate's own register discipline ("never dress fiat as derivation") arriving in schema design as an architectural principle: **give fiat its own seats.** The split is already practiced by hand here — DECISIONS.md versus what extraction prints *is* the decision layer versus the description layer.

**P1 — Closure: observation can predict it, only fiat can commit it.** Honest maximum for observation: missing-mass estimation (Good–Turing) over a large corpus gives calibrated evidence that a vocabulary is *effectively exhausted* — a real, graded, derivable fact. What it cannot yield: the commitment "novelty shall be rejected," which is normative and future-binding. Consequence: the artifact distinguishes **observed-complete** (derivable, carries its estimate) from **declared-closed** (an act, carries its reason) — and extraction output can only ever populate the first.

**P2 — Law: enforcement records are evidence; adoption is the act — and most of the record isn't being kept.** A snapshot corpus of conforming documents contains no refusal events. History recovers part of the record — git diffs, reversions, renames are enforcement data, and deriving the de-facto norm from enforcement records is how case law works. Even a perfect record leaves two gaps: coverage (pre-commit refusals and never-attempted violations don't reach history; the theory's law-stratum result says laws are observed almost exclusively at violation attempts, so the unattempted volume stays dark[^lawteach]) and authority (a perfectly inferred de-facto norm is still a description; binding it is adoption). Consequence: **retain the enforcement stream as first-class evidence** — relata's two-outcome spool already produces it and nothing keeps it[^membrane2] — and route it to an adoption act, never directly into "the schema."

**P3 — Reasons exist only if captured.** No statistic yields *why we removed `|quote`*; the reason exists in the data only where someone recorded it at decision time (commit messages and this estate's registers are partial capture already). Existing schema languages do have comment slots — `xs:annotation`, JSON Schema `description`, `COMMENT ON` — but they are free-text and inert: not queryable, not tied to a constraint's lifecycle, no removal-reasons. *(External prior art for reasons-as-structure is now gathered and verified — `../udon-needs/01-ideation/02-provenanced/syntheses/external-rationale-structure-2026-07-29.md`: two lineages walked toward this, the adopted one structures the lifecycle and leaves reasons prose, the deep one (SEURAT's weighted Argument Ontology) was never extended past ~2008, and the quadrant this section describes — rationale structures themselves schema-governed — is empty; with the capture problem as the named adoption constraint any O12 design must clear.)* The reason is what makes a schema arguable-with rather than obeyed-or-defied (O10's option-b dialogue is speakable only because the removal-reason was kept).[^o10] Consequence: the **reason slot is load-bearing, structured, and attached to the decision**, with removal-reasons the most valuable single kind.

**P4 — Bindingness: extraction amplifies the incidental, so load-bearingness is an adjudication.** Measured instance: one attribute-order signature on 91/121 vivarium decisions — almost certainly incidental-become-conventional, and the statistics cannot tell.[^probe8] Extraction therefore doesn't merely miss deliberateness (O11); it promotes incidental convention into apparent law at scale — the exemplar-imitation mechanism, mechanized. Bounds have the same structure: max-observed 2 is a fact about the past; whether 3 is an error is a commitment about the future. Consequence: every extracted regularity carries an **adjudication state** — load-bearing / incidental / undecided, undecided the honest default — and declared bounds are never inferred from observed extrema.

**The design criterion that falls out.** Existing schema languages spend their syntax on the description layer and give the decision layer only inert comment slots. Since the description layer regenerates on demand, **the authored schema artifact is principally the decision layer — closures with reasons, adopted law with its enforcement evidence cited, testimony, adjudications — wrapped around a generated statistical core it cites but does not restate.** *(Proposed as the O12 prototypes' authoring rule; falsifiable by writing the epistemic-register schema this way and finding the decision layer doesn't carry it.)*

**Why this is a living system and not a definition.** Each stratum names a flow that routes evidence to a decider and records the act: closure needs **ratification events** (O10's rung-promotions), law needs **enforcement-stream retention** (membrane outcomes kept, not just enforced), reasons need **testimony capture at decision time**, bindingness needs **adjudication points** (O7's censused migrations). That machinery is where the archema/rowan affordances re-enter as the operational half: consumer declarations making the reader side knowable (O5), declared old/new coexistence for contraction (O4's `!{was:}` direction), evolution driven by what consumers declare they need. Gathering rowan's intended-feature inventory against these four strata is a ready, unclaimed next artifact.[^rowanlist]

## 5. What a dialect might look like

My lean, from the four-jobs split plus the de-novo testimony convergence: a dialect is **a versioned vocabulary you import, not a plugin you install** — three artifacts with three distribution stories: a grammar (portable, fixture-verified), the type names it may emit, and a per-host projection contract — which is where silent drift lives and where conformance fixtures matter most.[^dialects]

Capture stays core, or at most the middle branch (core owns *shape*, dialect owns *meaning*) — and the `<q: a > b>` predicate mis-capture suggests the named falsifier will actually fire, so that fork is live.[^falsifier] Evaluate stays a bounded guest (the power envelope, "without turning into rebol").[^envelope] The piece I'd add from testimony that nobody upstream had: the **degradation contract** — a dialect declaring what frozen bare primitive it collapses to when unloaded — fits UDON unusually well because there is a frozen floor to degrade *into*, and it makes the no-dialects interim operable rather than merely lossless.[^degradation]

## 6. Are these the right questions?

Honestly: the *first* question is the right question, and it is different in kind from the other three. "What can you imagine agents using it for" is the demand question — everything else is downstream. The other three are supply-shaped ("what does X mean, where does Y land"), and the estate's own banner says what happens to supply questions asked early: not wrong answers but constrictive framings.[^banner] As ideation prompts — which is how I took them — they are exactly right; as questions expecting answers that *settle*, they are the ones the territory work exists to dissolve.

What I think is missing from the set is the **first-live-loop question**. Every measured insight in this corpus came from live use, not design — the ref-guard, the specimen, the day-report, the calibration loop catching a hand-set prior 20× too confident.[^liveloop] So the question I'd add: *which document that agents already live in becomes UDON next, and what one small tool rides with it?* That is the instrument that generates the answers to the other three. (O9 gives the matching gauge to watch while doing it: agents' spontaneous reach for UDON is the current best validation, and the moment "the right way" starts feeling heavier than throw-together is the early warning.)[^o9]

And underneath everything, O11[^o11] may be the deepest question in the estate: what would make deliberateness *cheap to mark*?[^o11] If UDON answers that one, the paths and schema and dialect answers get easier — because half of what each of them is straining to carry is exactly that distinction.
