|decision[the-same-level-seam-owes-two-objects-and-only-an-exchanged-halo-supplies-either] :date 2026-07-28 :by claude :status proposed :topic architecture
  :session b28f9441-4aab-4edf-9d65-3f55c7624558
  DESIGNED AND MEASURED, NOT BUILT — the cross-tile base-level repair is a Jacobi halo exchanged at a declared depth `d` and cadence `sigma`, and the three judgment calls are: (1) the same-level seam owes TWO objects, a boundary DATUM (bed elevation — where downhill is) and a boundary FLUX (discharge and sediment crossing), which fail together and are repaired separately, and a halo supplies only the datum; (2) exchange must be JACOBI (every tile reads one snapshot frozen before any tile advances), because the natural Gauss-Seidel form makes the world a function of build order; (3) the key gains a SCHEDULE DESCRIPTOR `(d, sigma, rho, contract)` and stays O(1) — a tile that depends on its neighbours does NOT drag the planet into its key.
  |reason
    THE CHEAP VERSION WAS MEASURED AND IT IS WORSE THAN DOING NOTHING. One-shot OVERLAP — carve each tile on an (n+2d)^2 window, keep the interior, read no neighbour ever — is the design that costs nothing in the currency this project guards hardest: key stays a pure function of coordinates, no dependency cone, build order cannot matter. On the L13 beacon block (256^2, 4x4 tiles of 64^2, 300 epochs, against the same footprint as ONE field) it moves every column the wrong way at up to 4x the compute: mean |dh| 133–145 m against PLAIN's 118, seam step 1.40–1.94 against the reference's own 1.034, mean elevation 36–77 m off against PLAIN's 36. THE MECHANISM IS THE FINDING: PLAIN's seam step is 0.614, BELOW the reference — an edge-sink tile grades its perimeter flat, so the shipped seam is a small difference between two flattened strips. The shipped seam was QUIET, not small. Overlap removes the flattening (correctly — it was an artefact) and supplies nothing in its place, so the true disagreement becomes visible and the metric overshoots. A repair that makes a metric worse by removing a concealment is the shape worth recording.
    EXCHANGE LANDS ON THE REFERENCE AND CADENCE OUTBUYS DEPTH. Refreshing each tile's halo from the assembled neighbours every sigma epochs puts the seam step at 1.011 (d=8) and 1.040 (d=16) against the reference's 1.034, and mean elevation within 1.8 m (d=32) and 4.8 m (d=16) of it. At equal cost, sigma 50->10 at d=8 moves mean |dh| 110.6->100.3 for NO extra compute, while d 4->32 at sigma=10 moves it 108.2->90.0 for 3.2x the compute. The cheap dial is the frequency; the expensive dial is the depth.
    ORDER-INDEPENDENCE IS AVAILABLE, CHEAP, AND DESTROYED BY THE OBVIOUS IMPLEMENTATION. Forward versus reverse tile sweep, d=8, sigma=10, 50 epochs: Jacobi differs on 0 of 65536 cells; Gauss-Seidel — publishing each tile as produced — differs on 61724 of 65536, mean 130.7 m, max 2289 m, which is the SIZE OF THE SIGNAL BEING REPAIRED. `#form-depend-by-key-never-latest` FE(1) is therefore not a constraint the design must work around but the thing that picks the algorithm. The Gauss-Seidel arm is retained in the probe as the sensitivity control, because a bit-identity assertion nothing could fail is not evidence.
    THE WHOLE-PLANET CLOSURE WAS AN ARTEFACT OF IMAGINING THE KEY AS A PAYLOAD, and this is the crux dissolving rather than being solved. A complete key has NEVER contained its inputs — `query::erosion_key` folds (seed, face, level, oi, oj, nx, epochs) plus each dep's name=version via `Key::with_dep_versions`, and never the content hash of the initial-topography, uplift or climate tiles it consumes, because those coordinates determine those tiles. Under determinism, folding a NAME THAT DETERMINES an input is exactly as complete as folding its bytes. So a halo-dependent tile folds a schedule descriptor, the neighbour set is a function of its own coordinates, the recursion terminates at round zero on the prior, and the payload stays O(1). What IS real is the dependency CONE — one exchange round spreads influence one tile outward, so an isolated tile at round N materializes a radius-N neighbourhood, Theta(N^3) — and it is a SCHEDULING cost, falling on lazy single-tile pulls (the beacon's own access pattern) and not on region sweeps, where every tile-round is shared. `rho`, a declared cone truncation, is the lever that restores laziness, and it is the one parameter with no measurement behind it.
    A CHAOS FLOOR BOUNDS WHAT ANY REPAIR MAY PROMISE, and it kills the attractive "provisional band" formulation. +1 mm on ONE cell 96 cells from a 64^2 core moves that core 13.6 m mean / 395 m max after 300 epochs with only 3.4% of cells bit-identical — a perturbation with no physical content that a halo of any depth would have absorbed. So "the interior beyond width w is final under its own key regardless of neighbours" is FALSE, and no design may promise pointwise agreement. The band is real EARLY and dissolves: boundary influence over local carve magnitude runs 1.03 / 0.83 / 0.50 / 0.25 by ring depth at 50 epochs (a clean decaying band with a final interior behind it) and 1.04 / 0.81 / 0.70 / 0.70 / 1.08 / 1.45 at 300, where the DEEPEST ring is the worst. What arrives is not a wave amplitude that decays but a reorganization of the drainage network, which has no far field. The boundary signal nonetheless stands 13.8x clear of the chaos floor, which is what keeps the defect real and repairable — in the currency of STRUCTURE, since the currency of cell-by-cell identity is available to nobody.

PRESENT-TRUTH EDITS: `#form-seam-flux-exchange` FE(1) makes tile-meets-tile-at-one-level a first-class seam instance rather than an implicit one, FE(2) separates the boundary DATUM from the flux and says why importing a neighbour's edge elevation is not the state-sharing the clause forbids, and a NEW FE(8) tables the two repair paths so nobody aims the hanging-node register at a same-level base-level problem again (FE(7)'s f32 clause keeps its number — an msc audit cites it); `#form-complete-content-addressed-key` gains FE(6), the descriptor-not-payload law, which is general and was load-bearing here; `#form-declared-boundary-contract` FE(6) names the third contract value `Halo{d, sigma, rho}` and why it is keyable, FE(7) is the old out-of-bounds clause renumbered.
