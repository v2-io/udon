Morning light has a way of rearranging a quiet room. Dust that was invisible at midnight suddenly hangs in the air like slow, drifting constellations, and the ordinary objects on a desk, a mug, a pen, a half-read book, take on a gentle significance, as if they had been waiting all night for someone to notice them. It is in these small, unhurried moments that the day's possibilities feel widest, before the noise of obligation arrives and the world narrows back into tasks and schedules.
