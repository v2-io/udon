Here is a short passage in LaTeX, where `\ref` fills in the figure number automatically:

```latex
\documentclass{article}
\usepackage{graphicx}

\begin{document}

Quarterly sales grew steadily over the past year, with the strongest
gains in the second half. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:sales-chart},
revenue rose in every quarter, and the sharpest increase occurred
between the third and fourth quarters.

\begin{figure}[ht]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{chart.png}
  \caption{Quarterly sales over the past year.}
  \label{fig:sales-chart}
\end{figure}

\end{document}
```

- `\label{fig:sales-chart}` must come after `\caption`, because the caption is what assigns the number.
- `\ref{fig:sales-chart}` resolves to that number (e.g., "Figure 1"). Compile twice so the reference updates.
- The `~` before `\ref` is a non-breaking space, which keeps "Figure" and its number on the same line.
- `chart.png` needs to be in the same directory as the `.tex` file, or you can give a path to it.

If you're using Markdown with Pandoc instead, the equivalent is:

```markdown
As shown in @fig:sales-chart, revenue rose in every quarter.

![Quarterly sales over the past year.](chart.png){#fig:sales-chart}
```

This requires `pandoc-crossref`.
