<span style="float:left;font-size:4.5em;line-height:0.8;font-weight:bold;margin:0.05em 0.1em 0 0;">T</span>he lighthouse at Gannet Point had been dark for eleven years when Maren Holt climbed its spiral stairs for the first time, carrying a borrowed lantern and a letter she had promised her father she would deliver. The brass rail was cold and slick with salt, and every iron step rang beneath her boots like a bell that had forgotten how to be struck gently. At the top, the great lens sat under a film of grey dust, and through the cracked glass she could see the harbor below, where the fishing boats rocked at anchor and a single light burned in the window of the Ashgrove house. Someone was waiting there. She had known that since she was a girl, though no one had ever told her so.
