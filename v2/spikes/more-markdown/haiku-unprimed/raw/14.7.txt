Gardens are quiet teachers. They reward patience and attention in a way few other pursuits do. A seed planted in spring asks for nothing more than water, light, and time, yet it repays the gardener with color, fragrance, and food. Working the soil by hand also slows the mind, turning a busy day into a series of small, deliberate tasks. Over time, the garden becomes less a place of chores and more a record of the seasons, each bloom marking a moment that was worth noticing.
