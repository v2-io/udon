**Why Bread Rises**

Bread rises because yeast consumes the sugars in flour and releases carbon dioxide gas. As the dough kneads and proofs, that gas becomes trapped in a web of gluten, the stretchy protein network that forms when wheat flour is mixed with water. The bubbles expand with heat in the oven until the crust sets, fixing the loaf's airy structure in place.

> **Sidebar: Did You Know?**
> Sourdough starters rely on wild yeast and bacteria rather than commercial yeast. Their sour flavor comes from the lactic and acetic acids the bacteria produce, which is why a well-kept starter can stay active for decades.
